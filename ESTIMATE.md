## Scope

**In.** Everything the disclosures flag as a safety, structural, or system failure; a full kitchen in its existing footprint with new fridge, range, sink, and dishwasher; a full bathroom in its existing footprint with a tiled shower and glass panel, new toilet, and new vanity; refinished floors throughout; and the work those items force (patching and painting the walls the rewire opens, permits, drawings).

**Out.** Window replacement, exterior paint, deck rebuild, rear porch conversion (it stays a laundry shed), driveway and EV charger, fireplace and chimney, wall insulation, yard and fence. These are the same deferrals as BUDGET.md and are still ~$80–150k in later years.

Rooms driving the numbers (from `floor-plan/generate.py`): kitchen 8′-2″ × 12′-5″ (101 sf, galley, roughly 20 LF of base cabinet on two walls with a 4′ aisle); bath 6′-8″ × 7′-7″ (51 sf); floors to refinish ≈ 440 sf (living 129, bedroom 2 93, bedroom 1 71, hall 23, closets 25, kitchen 101).

## What $250k actually buys

To land at the number already given to the lender, all of this has to happen at once:

- Every line at its low number. No surprises under the house, which the termite report says is unlikely.
- Seismic deferred to year two (−$9–15k). You lose the transfer-tax refund window.
- One mini-split head in the living room instead of three (−$5–7k). Bedrooms get space heaters until the next phase.
- Exterior rot repair held to deck stairs and anything structural (−$4–8k).
- Kitchen at IKEA boxes and mid-range appliances, bath with a prefab shower base instead of a tiled pan (−$5–8k combined).
- Contingency cut to 10% (bad idea on a 1919 house with active termites, but that is what the number requires).

That gets to roughly $250–265k and buys a sound, heated, rewired, replumbed house with a plain new kitchen and bath. It leaves no room for the crawlspace to be worse than the report, which is the single most likely outcome.

## Three things to settle before the offer

1. **The lender number.** The renovation loan appraises to after-repair value. Tiia's CMA (Sept 11) puts finished value at $900–950k. Purchase plus a $350k renovation is $1.0M before closing, above the ARV. At 80% loan-to-value on $925k the loan tops out around $740k, so cash needed is purchase plus renovation plus closing minus the loan, about $285k at the mid against ~$240k available per BUDGET.md. That is a $45k gap before a single surprise. Talk to Andy about the actual LTV and whether the appraiser will see $950k. This matters more than any line above.
2. **A real GC bid.** Everything above except $50k is an estimate. The lender needs a licensed GC's bid anyway; get two, on this scope, during the inspection contingency. Give them this file and `contractor-brief.html`.
3. **Termite "corrected" stamp and the rear half-bath.** Both could move the structural number. Ask the listing agent before the offer.

## Sequencing

Roof and gutters first. Termite, seismic, and sewer joints while the crawlspace is open. Then rewire, replumb, heat, and water heater in one pass with the kitchen and bath demoed so the walls are open. Insulation. Then kitchen and bath finishes, plaster and paint, floors last. Four to six months of construction after a Berkeley permit, which is running two to three months for plan check on a job this size.
